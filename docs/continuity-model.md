# Fluxlock Continuity Model

## Overview

Fluxlock replaces static identity with continuity-based identity.

Traditional systems define identity as possession.

Fluxlock defines identity as continuity.

Identity remains valid when continuity remains intact across time, evolution, and cryptographic rotation.

---

## Core Principle

Identity ≠ Keys

Identity ≠ Credentials

Identity = Continuity

---

## Continuity States

Validators exist in one of seven states.

### Healthy

Stable continuity.

Full participation rights.

---

### Evolving

Identity rotation or evolution occurring safely.

Continuity preserved.

---

### Recovering

Recent instability detected.

Trust rebuilding phase.

---

### Rehabilitating

Validator actively restoring legitimacy.

Partial participation restrictions.

---

### Quarantined

Continuity concerns present.

Network privileges reduced.

---

### Fractured

Major continuity failure detected.

Trust heavily reduced.

---

### Exiled

Continuity permanently rejected.

Validator removed from governance.

---

## Continuity Metrics

### Trust

Base confidence score.

Range:

0-100

---

### Continuity Reputation

Historical continuity performance.

---

### Adaptive Reputation

Ability to evolve safely.

---

### Continuity Memory

Accumulated history retained by the validator.

---

### Continuity Suspicion

Measures continuity uncertainty.

Higher values reduce legitimacy.

---

### Fracture Severity

Measures continuity damage.

---

## Continuity Chain

Every identity evolution creates a new lineage record.

Each record contains:

- Parent Hash
- Child Hash
- Epoch
- State
- Signature

Lineage forms an immutable continuity chain.

Preserve trust across change.

Allow evolution without identity loss.

Enable continuity-based authentication.
